- q: What plot sizes do you have?
  a: Plots start from 300 sq yards, which is about 2,700 sq ft. Other sizes depend on what is open today, so call {{phone1}} or {{phone2}} for the current list. We will tell you which plots are available and show their position on the layout plan.
- q: How big is 300 sq yards in feet?
  a: 300 sq yards is about 2,700 sq ft, or about 251 sq m, because one sq yard is nine sq ft. As a rough shape that is a plot near 50 ft by 54 ft. The real shape depends on the plot map, so ask to see it.
- q: What is the price of a plot?
  a: 'Price: Contact for price. Rates depend on size, position and payment plan. Call {{phone1}} or {{phone2}} and we will share today''s list, so you compare real numbers and not guesses.'
- q: How do I buy an industrial plot here?
  a: Call or message, visit the site, pick a plot on the layout plan, read the documents with your own advisor, then book and register.. Take your time between visit and booking, and never pay before you have read the papers.
- q: Which documents should I check before buying any industrial plot?
  a: Check the land use, the title and sale papers, the sanctioned layout plan, the plot number on that plan, and any dues. Ask your own lawyer or advisor to read them. This is a general checklist, not a statement about any one park.
- q: Can I use the plot for a factory or a warehouse?
  a: Plots are meant for industrial use such as small manufacturing and storage. Before you pay, confirm in the papers that your exact activity is permitted on that land, and ask your own advisor to read the wording. Do not rely on a verbal promise from any seller.
- q: Can I pay in instalments?
  a: Payment plans are still to be confirmed by the owner. Ask on the call, and get any plan in writing with dates and amounts before you book. Never pay on a verbal promise alone, and keep every receipt, whichever park you choose.
- q: What are the roads like?
  a: The park has 50 ft RCC roads. RCC is concrete, which stands up to loaded trucks better than a thin bitumen layer. A 50 ft width gives room to turn and for vehicles to pass. Check the surface and the turning space at your plot corner on your visit.
- q: Can trucks reach the plots?
  a: Yes, the park has 50 ft RCC roads, which are built for heavy vehicles. Still, check the turn into your own plot on a visit, using the size of vehicle you actually run. Bring a driver if you can, because they judge turning space best.
- q: Is there street lighting?
  a: Yes, the park has street lighting along its roads, which helps night shifts and late loading. On your visit, look at the poles, the spacing and whether they switch on at dusk. Ask how many are installed and who looks after them.
- q: Is there greenery in the park?
  a: Yes, the park has planted greenery along its roads. It gives shade, helps with dust a little and makes the place better for workers and visitors. Ask on your visit what is planted, how it is watered and what more is planned.
- q: What about power, water and drainage?
  a: 'These details are still to be confirmed by the owner, so do not rely on a web page for them. Ask for written details on the call: power load and who supplies it, water source and supply hours, and where waste water goes. Then see each one on your site visit.'
