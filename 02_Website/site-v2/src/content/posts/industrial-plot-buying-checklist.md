## The short answer on buying a plot

Shortlist two or three parks, visit with your biggest vehicle, ask the same questions everywhere, have your own advisor read the papers, compare the total cost, and book in writing.

## Moves 1 and 2: shortlist and visit

- **Shortlist plots.** Pick two or three parks on one road or belt. Get plot sizes and rates in writing, the road width, the road distance to your highway, and what is on site today versus promised.
- **Visit the site.** Bring your most important vehicle and one other person. Check the gate and the main road, the surface and turns inside, the lighting, and the plot position on the layout plan. Stand on the plot, check its boundaries, and photograph it with the date.

## Moves 3 and 4: questions and papers

- **Ask.** What is the exact area and where are the boundaries? What use is permitted for my activity? What power load, from whom, and when? Where does waste water go? What charges come beyond the plot rate? What is the payment plan?
- **Verify.** Give copies to your own lawyer: land use and the seller's right to sell, the sanctioned layout plan, the sale papers, and any dues or disputes. If anything is unclear, wait. A delay costs less than a dispute.

## Moves 5 and 6: cost and booking

- **Compare.** For each park add the plot price, registry and stamp duty, development or maintenance charges, power connection, boundary wall and shed, drainage and shifting. Compare totals, not headline rates.
- **Book the plot.** Book in writing with the plot number, area, rate, payment dates and documents. Keep every receipt and never pay on a promise. Go twice, at different hours, before you decide.
