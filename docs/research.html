What we studied before building.

Brand kit anatomy, rooted Indian brands, hoarding legibility and file formats. Short version below; full reading list at the end.

1

What a brand kit contains

SectionWhat it contains
Logo systemLockups, clear space, minimum size, one-colour and reversed versions.
ColourScreen and print values, usage balance, contrast pairs.
TypographyTwo typefaces at most and how each is used.
Graphic elementsThe one or two recurring shapes that make the brand recognisable: here the sunburst and the stripe.
TemplatesReal layouts at real sizes: boards, hoardings, cards, letterhead, social.
RulesWhat never changes, what is safe to change, who approves, what to check before print.

Consensus across the guidelines reviewed: put the most recognisable assets first, define each value once, and write it so someone who has never met the brand can use it correctly.

2

What rooted Indian brands show

McDonald's India

Fixed visual core, local everything else

Arches, red and yellow, packaging and layout stay uniform while the menu and messages adapt.

Bata India

A colour taken from the logo

Red and white stores mirror the logo; the name is written in local scripts. Trust compounds over decades.

boAt

Identity, not specifications

Case studies describe lifestyle, a named community and bold design rather than technical talk.

Vodafone poles

Recognised from far away

Your own reference: one colour repeated on every pole reads at distance.

3

Rules applied to the boards

Few words

Seven words or fewer, simple sans-serif, high contrast, generous empty space. So each board carries the logo, one line and two numbers.

Test at scale

Letter-height rules range from 1 in per 10 ft to 1 in per 30 ft of viewing distance. Check at full size from the approach lane.

True vector

Print needs vector. Tracing a PNG is not the same as an original vector logo, so keep looking for the original.
