Research

How a brand kit is built, and what makes a brand stick.

Before drawing anything we studied what a working brand kit contains, how a board should be presented, and the repeatable mechanics behind brands that became part of everyday India.

1 · Anatomy

Consensus across the guidelines we reviewed: logo with clear space and minimum size, colour with digital and print values, typography, imagery, applications and misuse. Voice is optional in some kits and included here because the road is a place where words matter.

2 · Presenting to a board

The order that gets a decision

1

The situation

The road, the competitors, the gap.

2

The directions

Three ideas, each with a one-line reason.

3

The proof

Real photos, true scale, measured colour.

4

The decision

Scorecard, size question, phases, tiers.

What a pitch should not claim: approvals, utilities, road widths, plot availability, prices or investment returns that have not been signed off. This deck uses none.

3 · Rooted brands

Four Indian brands and the mechanic each shows

McDonald's India

A fixed visual core, local everything else.

The arches, the red and yellow, packaging and store layout stay uniform, while the menu and communication are localised. Trade press records that early on the arches meant nothing to Indian customers, so signs needed words like 'Family Restaurant'.

What we took: Keep the logo and colour identical everywhere; let words and offers flex.

Bata India

Colour taken straight from the logo, then repeated until it is the shop.

Red and white stores mirror the logo, and the name is written in Hindi, Bengali, Odia and other scripts where Bata trades. Trust compounds over decades.

What we took: Write the name in Hindi as well as English, and keep one colour pair as the brand's skin.

boAt

Sells identity, not specifications.

Cases describe a brand built on lifestyle, a named community ('boAtheads'), influencers and bold youth-led design rather than technical talk.

What we took: Give the road a voice and a community word; do not list features on a board.

Kofi Kafi

One saturated colour, one face, hyper-local naming.

No published case study was found. From your photograph: a single deep blue light-box, a white wordmark with a smile curve, the outlet named after the place ('Vishwavidyalay'), the menu visible through the glass. We treat this as an observation, not research.

What we took: One colour, one symbol, name the place. A board you can recognise from the far end of the street.

Five mechanics we applied to IIP

  1. Own a colour nobody around you owns (Jamun, Gulaal).
  2. One symbol, never redrawn: the rosette stays exactly as drawn.
  3. One repeated sentence that people can say back to you.
  4. Say it in Hindi and write the name in both scripts.
  5. Consistent placement: the same board at the same pole height, at every pole.
4 · How this was made

Method and honesty notes

  • The logo is the supplied artwork, cleaned to a transparent master. Nothing redrawn.
  • Every board, hoarding and gate is exact artwork placed into photographs, so the logo and text are never re-drawn by AI.
  • The blank scenes (hoarding, gate) were generated with ChatGPT image generation from your own roadside photo as the lighting reference, with a neutral-light, unedited-phone-photo prompt, then the artwork was warped into the blank panel keeping the panel's own shading.
  • The pole mockups use your own photographs at true scale (pole face about 10 inch).
  • Colour distance is measured with CIEDE2000 on colours sampled from your photographs.
  • Public search found no authoritative description of IIP or its Phase 1 and Phase 2. We used the brief and the Google Maps listing.
  • Competitor claims (approvals, prices, road widths) are what their boards say; none verified.
  • No cost figures are quoted because none were supplied; the decision page gives structure and cost drivers.
