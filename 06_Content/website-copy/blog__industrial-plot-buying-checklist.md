## The short answer

Buy an industrial plot in five moves: shortlist, visit, verify the papers, compare the total cost, then book. Do not pay before your own advisor has read the documents and you have stood on the exact plot. This checklist works for any park, including ours.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm each point with your advisor and the relevant authority.

## Move 1: Shortlist

Pick two or three parks on the same road or belt. Write down for each:

- Plot sizes and rates, in writing.
- Road width and surface inside the park.
- Distance by road to your main highway, and the time.
- What is on site today versus what is promised.

Ask the same questions of every park so you compare like with like.

## Move 2: Visit the site

Plan a visit with your most important vehicle and one other person. On site:

1. **Gate and main road.** Is the entry wide enough for your vehicle? Can two vehicles pass?
2. **Internal road.** Look at the surface, the edges, standing water and the turn into your plot.
3. **Lighting.** Note poles, spacing and whether they light at dusk.
4. **Plot position.** Ask to see the plot on the layout plan, then stand on it. Check corners and boundaries.
5. **Around the park.** Note the neighbouring land use and how far the nearest highway ramp is.
6. **People and activity.** Is anyone working on site? What is already built?

Take photos and write the date on them.

## Move 3: Ask these questions

- What is the exact area of my plot and where are the boundaries marked?
- What is the permitted use of this land for my activity?
- What power load can I get, from whom, and in how long?
- Where does waste water go and what consent is needed for my work?
- What are the charges beyond the plot rate (registry, development, maintenance)?
- What is the payment plan and what happens if I miss a date?
- Which documents will I receive and when can I read them?

Write the answers down. Ask for anything important in writing.

## Move 4: Verify the papers

Give copies to your own lawyer or advisor. They should read:

- The land use and the right of the seller to sell.
- The sanctioned layout plan and your plot number on it.
- The sale papers and the registry steps.
- Any dues, loans or disputes against the land.
- Anything the seller promises in a brochure or on a call: ask to see it in the agreement.

If anything is unclear, wait. A delay costs less than a dispute.

## Move 6: Book

When you are satisfied:

- Book in writing, with the plot number, area, rate, payment dates and the documents you will receive.
- Keep every receipt.
- Register the sale as your advisor guides.
- Ask for the plot to be shown on site on the day of possession.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Paying on a promise.** If it is not in writing, treat it as not promised. Brochures and call notes are not an agreement.
- **Judging by one visit.** Go twice, at different hours, and bring someone who has not been convinced yet.
- **Comparing rates only.** A lower rate with higher extras can cost more in the end. Fill the cost table for every park.
- **Ignoring the turn.** Many buyers check the plot and forget to check how a loaded truck enters it.
- **Skipping the advisor.** Your own lawyer or advisor costs little against the price of a plot. Ask them to read everything before the money moves.
- **Forgetting growth.** Buy for the unit you will have in five years, or make sure you can add a neighbouring plot.
