## The short answer

Choose an industrial plot by working backwards from your trucks and your next five years. Fix the plot size first, then check the road in front of it, how easily you reach a highway, whether power and drainage are in writing, and whether the papers are clean. Visit at least twice before you pay.

## Step 1: Decide how much land you really need

Start from what you make and how you move it, not from the price.

- **Machines and work area.** List every machine and the clear space around it. Add space for raw material and finished goods.
- **Loading side.** A truck needs room to arrive, turn and leave. A plot that is wide but has no turning space will cost you every day.
- **Growth.** Add room for a second shed or a line extension. Moving later is expensive.
- **Rules of thumb.** A 300 sq yard plot is about 2,700 sq ft (roughly 50 ft by 54 ft). That fits a small workshop, light assembly or storage. A heavier unit with larger machines usually needs more. Ask your equipment supplier for the footprint before you decide.

## Step 2: Look at the road, not just the plot

The road in front of the plot decides how easy every delivery is.

- **Width.** A road that allows a loaded truck to turn and two vehicles to pass saves time. Our own park has 50 ft RCC roads, so ask any park you visit for the exact widths and see them.
- **Surface.** Concrete (RCC) holds loaded trucks better than a thin bitumen layer. Look for cracks, broken edges and standing water.
- **Corner turn.** Bring the biggest vehicle you use, or measure its turning circle, and check the turn into your plot.

## Step 3: Check how you reach the highway

Count the minutes from the plot to the road your trucks will mostly use.

- Ask for distance by road, not straight line. A straight-line figure is always shorter than the drive.
- Drive the route at the time your trucks would run. Note tolls.
- Note the distance to the town where your workers and suppliers are.

For example, from Isapur Industrial Park the Delhi-Meerut Expressway on-ramp at Bhojpur is 4.7 km by road, about 8 minutes (Google Maps, 6 November 2026). Times change with traffic, so do your own test drive for any park.

## Step 4: Power, water and drainage

Do not accept "available" as an answer. Ask for the details in writing.

- **Power.** What load can you get, from whom, and how long does a connection take?
- **Water.** Source, supply hours and cost.
- **Drainage.** Where does waste water go? Industrial waste water normally has rules. Ask which consent applies to your activity and whether the park helps.

If a park cannot show a clear answer, count that as a risk and price it in.

## Step 5: Read the papers with your own advisor

This is a general guide, not legal advice. Before you pay anything, have your own lawyer or advisor read the documents.

- Land use for industrial activity.
- Title and the seller's right to sell.
- The sanctioned layout plan and your plot number on it.
- Any dues, loans or disputes on the land.
- The exact area and boundaries of your plot.

## Step 6: Count the total cost

The plot price is only one line.

- Registry and stamp duty.
- Development or maintenance charges, if any.
- Power connection and transformer costs.
- Boundary wall, shed, flooring and drainage inside your plot.
- Your own cost of shifting machines.

Put these into one sheet. A cheaper plot with higher extra costs can end up dearer.

## Step 7: Visit twice

Go once in the day with questions and once at a different hour to see how the site looks and how the road works when vehicles are moving. Take photos and notes. Ask to see the plot position on the layout plan and stand on the plot.

## The checklist

- [ ] I know my machine layout and the area I need, with growth room
- [ ] I know the biggest vehicle that will enter and have checked the turn
- [ ] I have seen the road width and surface, not just heard about it
- [ ] I know the road distance and time to my main highway, and I drove it
- [ ] Power load, water and drainage details are written down
- [ ] My own advisor has read the land and layout documents
- [ ] I have a sheet with the total cost, not just the plot rate
- [ ] I visited at least twice and took photos
- [ ] I compared at least two parks on the same list
