# IIP voice guide (living)

Rule of the file: the owner's reaction beats this guide. Every reaction is logged in FEEDBACK-LOG.md and this guide is updated the same day. Last update: 2026-11-13 (seeded).

## 1. Who we talk to
Factory owners, workshop owners, warehouse and godown operators and investors along the Hapur-Modinagar Road. Local, practical, busy, burned by hype before. They read a billboard at 40 km/h, a brochure at a desk, a post in a spare minute. English only.

## 2. One-line voice
A neighbour who knows the road, speaks plainly, smiles a little, and shows you the plot instead of selling it.

## 3. Tone dials (1 to 5)
| Dial | Setting | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Plain vs fancy | 1 | Words a shop-floor reader uses. |
| Warm vs cold | 4 | Talks to one person ("you", "your trucks"). |
| Playful vs serious | 2 | A light smile now and then. One playful line per piece at most. Never joke about money, land papers or safety. |
| Concrete vs abstract | 1 | A number, a road, a truck, a shop. Picture first. |
| Boast vs modest | 2 | Quiet pride, never superlatives. |
| Legal vs human | 1 | No disclaimers inside the line. Proof lives on a separate papers page and in the owner's files. |

## 4. Do
- Lead with the thing a buyer can picture: a loaded truck turning, a lit road at night, a tyre shop at the gate.
- Short sentences. Active verbs. One idea per line.
- Say who it is for ("Shops for the people who serve the factories").
- Use only facts in section 8. A number appears only when its source is on file.
- Give one next step: call, WhatsApp, visit 8 am to 8 pm, closed Tuesday.
- One tagline per creative, never both.
- Warm without gush. Playful without a pun parade.

## 5. Do not
- Disclaim, hedge or explain fine print inside marketing lines ("not a promise that...", "subject to", "may vary", "terms apply" in a headline or body line).
- Hype: best, premium, world-class, unmatched, dream, once in a lifetime, hurry, limited.
- Stiff or official wording: "Counts are plots in the three layouts", "kindly note", "as per".
- Invent proof: no water, security, permitted uses, price, percentages, returns, brand names, developer name, the word resale, "100 acres".
- Two taglines in one piece. Em dashes anywhere.
- Bait questions, fake curiosity hooks, "not X, just Y", "no fluff", lists of "no A, no B, no C".
- Hindi or Hinglish (English only, until the owner says otherwise).

## 6. Sentence rules
- 12 words or fewer for headlines, 18 for body. One comma at most per line.
- Prefer nouns you can touch (road, light, tree, pump, gate, shop) over nouns you cannot.
- Numerals ("50 ft", "4.2 km"), not words.
- Billboards: 7 words or fewer, counted. Logo and phone are outside the count.
- Read it aloud. If a plot buyer would not say it on the phone, rewrite it.
- State a fact once. No echo, no "that means".

## 9. Structure by channel
- Web: one headline (12 words or fewer), one line under it (18 or fewer), one proof fact, one button. Headings say the thing. SEO terms (industrial plots, Hapur-Modinagar Road, Modinagar) go in H2s and first lines, naturally. Never override page title, meta or schema owned by the build.
- Billboard: 7 words or fewer. One idea. Phone big. Logo. Tagline optional, one at most.
- Banner: 7 to 12 words plus phone. One fact.
- Brochure: a headline, a 2 line intro, 5 short fact blocks, a how-to-visit block, phone, WhatsApp, address. One page.
- Instagram: 15 to 35 words, one image idea, 3 to 6 hashtags from real places and the trade. LinkedIn: 40 to 80 words, same voice, a touch more detail, no thought-leader cadence. End with one action or none.

## 10. Learned rules (grows from FEEDBACK-LOG.md)
1. No defensive or legal wording in copy. Proof goes on the papers page. (owner, 2026-11-13)
2. Plain, warm, a little playful, meaningful, concrete, never hype. (owner, 2026-11-13)
3. Say who the thing serves. Put the people in the line. (from "Shops for the people who serve the factories")
4. Buying and sales pages are salesy: confident, benefit-first, a little polished and aspirational, still concrete, never hype. Never say "we get to know you and your industry". The first meeting is us showing what is for sale and walking buyers through the park and the papers. (owner, 2026-11-14)
5. Never say payments or cash flow through IIP, or that our name is on or stands behind every sale. Help-you-sell-later stays, with no payment clause. (owner, 2026-11-14)
6. Approval claims cover Phase 2 and Phase 3 only, worded "Approved by Zila Panchayat Ghaziabad". Never "map", "layout map", "stamped map", "sanctioned", "subject to conditions", or any Phase 1 claim. (owner, 2026-11-14)
